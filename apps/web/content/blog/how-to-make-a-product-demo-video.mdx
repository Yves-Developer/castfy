## Why demos are usually slow to make

A good demo takes longer than it should. You write a script, record your screen, fumble a click, record again, then spend an hour in an editor cutting pauses, adding zoom and recording a voiceover. When the app changes next month, you do it all again.

Most of that time goes on things a computer can do: clicking through a flow it has been told about, waiting less, and reading out what is happening. That is the part Castfy hands to your coding agent.

## What you need

- **Castfy**, the desktop app, for Mac, Windows or Linux.
- **A coding agent** you already use. Claude Code is the one Castfy is tested with; OpenCode, Codex and the Cursor CLI work too, in experimental support. Castfy runs it on your own plan or key, so a demo spends quota you already pay for.
- **A URL**. It can be a live site, a staging server, or `localhost` while you are still building.

## Tips for a prompt that records well

- **Start where the demo starts.** Give the exact page, not the homepage, unless the homepage is the point.
- **Name the things to click.** "Click Export, then PNG" records better than "export the drawing".
- **Say where to end.** A clear last screen gives the demo a clean ending.
- **Keep it to one idea.** A 30–60 second demo of one flow is easier to watch and to share than a three-minute tour.
- **Signed-in apps work.** The browser runs on your machine, so you can sign in once and the agent carries on from there.
